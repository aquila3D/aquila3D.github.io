Network Meta-Analysis: Venous Leg Ulcer Treatment
M1 Coursework, 2026
R + French

Bayesian hierarchical network meta-analysis of 15 randomized controlled trials comparing 6 treatment strategies for venous leg ulcers. Implemented random-effects models via MCMC simulation with convergence diagnostics (Gelman-Rubin, trace plots) and probabilistic ranking of treatments via SUCRA.

Key Results: Laser therapy shows 42.8% probability of being most effective (52% in fixed-effects model). Analysis identified moderate heterogeneity (I² = 46%) requiring random-effects specification. Full Bayesian posterior inference and sensitivity analyses included.
Methods: Bayesian hierarchical modeling · MCMC simulation (WinBUGS) · Convergence diagnostics · SUCRA probabilistic rankings · Network evidence synthesis

Predictive Classification: Cancer Survival Outcomes (OECD)
M1 Coursework, 2026
R + French

Supervised machine learning classification project predicting net survival at 5 years for 1,455 cancer patients across 46 OECD countries, 2004-2014. Implemented and compared three algorithms: CART decision trees, Random Forest ensemble, and multinomial logistic regression.

Key Results: Decision tree (CART) achieved 91.7% accuracy (Kappa = 0.87). Cancer type is the dominant predictor (explains ~95% of model variance). Class-specific sensitivity: Low survival 95.9%, Medium 89.8%, High survival 89.1%. Model validated via 5-fold cross-validation with stratified sampling.
Methods: Supervised classification · Decision trees (CART) · Random Forest · Multinomial logistic regression · Cross-validation · Feature importance · Confusion matrices

Health Financing & Population Outcomes: Multi-Country Panel Analysis
Research in Progress, 2026
R

Comparative econometric analysis of 31 Latin American and Caribbean countries (2015-2022) testing competing hypotheses: Does total health spending commitment (% GDP) or financing structure (out-of-pocket burden) more strongly predict population health outcomes? Panel analysis with multiple model specifications and rigorous diagnostics.

Key Findings: Health expenditure as % GDP is a robust predictor across all outcomes (β = 0.94 years life expectancy, p = 0.001; β = -2.92 infant deaths per 1,000, p = 0.002). Out-of-pocket burden shows no independent effect (p > 0.15 across outcomes) once total spending is controlled. Hausman test strongly rejects random-effects model (χ² = 45.03, p < 0.001), validating fixed-effects specification.
Methodology: Pooled OLS with year fixed effects · Random-effects model · Fixed-effects (within estimator) · Lagged specifications for temporal dynamics · Hausman specification test · Sensitivity analyses (regional subsets, COVID exclusion) · Multicollinearity diagnostics (VIF < 2.0 all variables) · Five outcome specifications
